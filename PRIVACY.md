# How GRAVITAS reads your journal, and how to check it yourself

GRAVITAS's code does not upload or store your Elite Dangerous journal. This page says exactly what it reads, what it sends and when, where that happens in the code, and how you can watch it in your own browser.

## In short

- Your browser reads the journal files itself. The files are not uploaded.
- GRAVITAS asks the browser for **read-only** access to the folder you choose. It cannot write, change or delete anything there.
- What goes out because of your journal is the **name of a star system**, so the map can show it. That is the same request as typing the name in the search box.
- Combat reports and settings stay in your own browser. GRAVITAS's server does not receive them, except the parts you choose to share (below).
- Sharing with your own phone, or with your team, happens only when you switch it on. What goes where is listed below.

## What Sync and Combat Mode read

Where you are and where you jump, your route and jump range, your carriers, your target, where the bodies you scan are on their orbits (for Collisions, kept only while the page is open), and for Combat Mode the fight: the ships you scan and their bounty, your kills, the attacks on you, your loadout, your combat rank, your team's names, the active Operation, and what NPCs say to you. Of your team's chat, only how many lines each one wrote is counted, never the words. All of it is read and kept in your browser's memory.

## Where it happens in the code

| Step | File · function | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| You choose the folder | `src/js/30-sync.js` · `syncDialog` | `showDirectoryPicker({ mode: "read" })`: the browser asks you, and gives read-only access |
| The folder is remembered | `src/js/30-sync.js` · `syncDB` | Keeps the folder **handle** (the permission, not the files) in the browser's own IndexedDB, so you don't choose it again |
| The newest journal is found | `src/js/30-sync.js` · `syncScan`, `syncNewest` | Lists the folder in your browser |
| New lines are read every second | `src/js/30-sync.js` · `syncTick` | `file.slice(...).text()`: reads only the new part of the file, in memory |
| Each line is understood | `src/js/30-sync.js` · `syncLine`, `src/js/42-combat.js` · `cbEvent`, `src/js/41-own-carrier.js` · `fcEvent` | Picks out the things listed above. The rest of the line is ignored |
| The map follows you | `src/js/30-sync.js` · `syncFollow` → `src/js/23-data.js` · `loadSystem` | Asks `/api/system?name=<system>&lite=1`. Only the system's name |
| Your scans set the orbits | `src/js/30-sync.js` · `scanSeen`, `src/js/29-coll-engine.js` · `scanFix` | From a Scan line: the body's number and its place on its orbit (MeanAnomaly), in memory for this tab only. Not stored, not sent |
| Combat reports | `src/js/42-combat.js` · `cbSave` | `localStorage` in your browser, the last 30. Not sent |

The server side is in this repository too:
- `functions/api/system.js`: takes a system name (or Spansh's number for it), asks Spansh, answers. Spansh's answer for that system is kept in Cloudflare's cache for 15 minutes, shared by everyone. Nothing about who asked is kept with it.
  With `&lite=1` (how the site asks since v0.99.3) the answer leaves out the stations' market, outfitting and shipyard lists (`lib/slim.js`), so it is much smaller. The page asks for the first system as soon as it opens (a small script in `index.html`, the same request as the search box would make). When you open a station, the Station window asks once for the same system in full (`/api/system?id64=<number>`, `src/js/37-station.js` · `stwFull`) to show those lists. Only the system's name or number is sent.
- `functions/api/autocomplete.js`: the search box's suggestions, cached the same way by the text typed.
- `functions/api/room.js` and `worker/src/room.js`: the phone pairing and the team board (see below).
- `functions/api/report.js`: problem reports, only when you send one.

## Only when you switch it on

| Feature | What goes out | Kept? |
|---|---|---|
| A phone or tablet as a second screen (`src/js/33-pair.js`, `33-pair-manage.js`; v0.99.11: a browser can also take up the app's pairing with a one-time link code) | The short "where am I" summary the Sync card shows, and in a fight the combat screen, to **your** paired devices. Each device tells the room what kind it is ("Android phone · Chrome"), so every place that holds your pairing can list and remove it (v0.99.10, see "Devices" below) | The server keeps the latest "where am I" summary for up to 12 hours after the last device leaves. A device's kind, join time and last check-in live only with its open connection. The combat screen is passed on and not kept (see the note below) |
| Team board (`src/js/43-opboard.js`) | Your CMDR name, ship, kills, credits and top kill, to the people who have the board's link | Not kept: your row lives only on your open connection |
| Report a problem (`src/js/27-report.js`) | What you write, your screenshots, and the details shown in the report window | Sent by e-mail to the developers |

**Pairing safety (v0.99.9).** Making a pairing also makes a random host key. Your computer keeps it (in this browser) and the room keeps only a one-way hash of it, for as long as the room lives. Only the holder of that key can act as the computer for that pairing. The 6-digit code works once (the same address may repeat it for 90 seconds, for a phone that reloads) and is gone after five minutes at most. To slow down code guessing, the server counts code lookups and new pairings per address for ten minutes: the address is kept only as a one-way code, in Cloudflare's cache at the data centre that answered, and is gone when the ten minutes end. What a computer passes on to its devices is checked and cut to the fields the screens use before it is forwarded.

**Devices, the same list everywhere (v0.99.10).** A pairing can be held by more than one place at once: the site in a browser tab, the GRAVITAS MAP window of the app, and the app's Modes page (`src/js/33-pair-manage.js`, `app/src-tauri/src/pairing.rs`). What is held is the pairing's room name and its host key, and nothing else. Each holder asks `https://gravitasmap.com/api/room` for the list of devices (`do=status`), and can remove one device (`do=kick`) or end the pairing for everybody (`do=stop`). The host key travels only in the `X-Host-Key` header of these calls and, for the live WebSocket link (a browser cannot set headers on a WebSocket), as its sub-protocol `gravitas.hk.<key>`, so it is not in the address. One exception, written down on purpose: if the link cannot be opened twice in a row that way (a proxy that drops sub-protocols), the page falls back to the older `?hk=<key>` address so sharing keeps working, and then the key is in that address like before v0.99.10. Our code never writes the key to a log. The answer lists, per device: a random 8-character id, the kind of device it said it is ("Android phone · Chrome", cut to 40 characters, markup removed), whether it checked in during the last 40 seconds, when it joined, and whether it is linked directly or through the server. It never contains the host key or its hash. **What the room keeps for this:** the kind, the join time and the last check-in time of each device live with that device's open connection and are gone when the device leaves (they are not written to storage). Ending a pairing removes every device, closes every link, deletes the key's hash and the last summary, and leaves a one-line "stopped" mark until the room is forgotten (12 hours). To slow abuse the server counts these calls per address for ten minutes (900 calls, and 20 calls with a wrong key, counted apart; the address is kept only as a one-way code in Cloudflare's cache, as for codes). While a page that holds the key has the Devices dialog open it asks every 5 seconds, otherwise every 15 to 60 seconds (a page with an open link to the room is told about changes by the room itself). The app's Modes page asks only while that page is showing and the window is visible. **Removing a device only disconnects it:** whoever still has the link or the code can follow again, so "Stop sharing" (and then setting it up again) is what locks everything. Only one place at a time sends "where am I" to the devices (the room keeps one such computer, a second replaces the first); the others keep showing and managing the list.
**Where the key lives.** In a browser: in that site's own browser storage, as before. In the app: the pairing is made by the GRAVITAS MAP window (the site's code makes the room, the code and the QR). That window keeps it in its page memory only (not in its web storage), and the app keeps a copy in its `settings.json` (key `pairing`: room and host key, in plain text like the rest of that file; it is also in the second copy of settings in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\GRAVITAS-backup`). While the map window is open, every 2 seconds the app looks at what the window holds (WebView2 `ExecuteScript`, the answer comes back to the app: the page can call nothing in the app and the window has no capability), and when a map window is opened the app hands the key over to it with `eval`, only if the window shows `gravitasmap.com`. The app talks only to `https://gravitasmap.com` for this (a development map window on another address is not looked at). **Open in browser** (Modes page): opens `https://gravitasmap.com/#host=<room>.<key>` in your default browser. What follows the `#` is never sent to a server by a browser; the page reads it once, stores the pairing in that browser's storage and removes it from the address bar at once. It can still be seen on your computer: in the command line Windows starts your browser with, and, for a moment, in the browser's history entry before the page cleans it (a browser that syncs history may keep that entry). Treat that address like a password and do not share it. A pairing made in a browser tab on the same computer is not seen by the app unless you open it from the app this way, or link that browser with a code (next paragraph).

**Link code (v0.99.11).** A normal browser (any browser, any profile) can take up the pairing the app holds, without a link that carries the key. In the app (Modes, Phones and tablets) you press **Link a browser**. The app sends one POST to `https://gravitasmap.com/api/room?room=<room>&do=linkcode` with the host key in the `X-Host-Key` header (never in the address), and shows the code the server answers. The code is **8 characters from a 32-character alphabet** (no I, O, 0, 1), shown as `ABCD-EFGH`, which is 40 bits. It lives **2 minutes** and works **once**. On gravitasmap.com you press **Link with the GRAVITAS app** (Sync card) and type it; the page sends one POST to `/api/room?do=link` with the code in the `X-Link-Code` header (not in the address) and, on success, is handed the pairing's room name and host key once, in an answer that is marked `Cache-Control: no-store`. The page then stores them in that browser's own storage exactly like a pairing taken up from the `#host=` address, and shows the same device list. **What the server holds, and for how long:** the room keeps only the one-way hash of the key, as before, so it cannot hand the key out by itself. For the two minutes a code lives, a small object named after the code holds the room name and the key (in plain form, in Cloudflare's own storage, which is encrypted at rest) so it can give them to the one who types the code. That record is deleted at the first successful use, when the two minutes end, and by the object's alarm; a pairing that is stopped has no live code (a code for it is refused even if it was never used). A wrong, an already used, an expired and a stopped pairing's code all get the same answer ("not valid"), so the server does not tell which it was. A code only has to be guessed once in about a million million: to slow guessing, the server counts **wrong codes per address, 5 per ten minutes**, after which that address gets "too many tries" even for the right code until the ten minutes end, and the address may ask for at most 10 new codes per ten minutes. The address is kept as a one-way code only, in Cloudflare's cache, as for the other limits above. The browser must send a request from gravitasmap.com itself (a foreign page is refused). **What is logged: nothing.** Our code writes no log line about a code or a key, and the code is in a header, not in an address, so it is not in an address log. **Who can take the pairing:** whoever types a live code first, so show the code only to yourself and use it within the two minutes. A browser that holds a pairing this way is told, when the app later ends that pairing (for example with a new link), "The pairing was ended. Link again with the GRAVITAS app.", and forgets its copy. Removing a browser's copy of the key is not possible from the app: **Stop sharing** (then set it up again) is what locks every holder out.

**App window (v0.99.12).** The App button in the Sync card opens a page about the GRAVITAS app. Only when you open it, the page reads small public files from this same site: `/gravitas-app/version.json` (the latest installer, its size and its SHA-256, the same signed file the app reads), `/gravitas-app/scan.json` (the date of the last antivirus scan of that exact installer, written when the release was made) and `/api/scan` (the current public VirusTotal result for that installer). `/api/scan` is answered by our server, which asks virustotal.com about the installer's SHA-256 (a file hash, nothing about you) with our own key about every 6 hours per server location (each location keeps its own cache) and keeps the answer; your browser never contacts VirusTotal. Nothing about you is sent, no cookie is set, and the VirusTotal link, when it shows, only opens virustotal.com if you click it.

**A note on earlier versions.** From v0.91 to v0.91.7, with a phone or tablet paired, the combat screen was kept in the pairing room together with the "where am I" summary, for up to 12 hours after the last device left. It held your CMDR name, your kills and your team's names. It was not your journal, but our privacy text did not say so. We found it ourselves and changed it in v0.92: since then the combat screen is only passed on.

## GRAVITAS for EDMC (the plugin, retired)

The EDMC plugin is retired: it is no longer developed or distributed, and the GRAVITAS app replaced it. While it existed it ran inside E:D Market Connector on your computer, read the Journal and Status.json read-only from the game's own folder, worked out the fight locally and opened no network connection. Its source is kept only in this repository's history.

## The GRAVITAS app (Windows)

The app reads your Journal and Status.json on your own computer and shows the Combat windows over the game. Nothing it reads is sent anywhere by the app's own code.
The app's own network calls (its Rust code) are the update check and, when an update is installed, the download of the signed installer, both from `https://gravitasmap.com/gravitas-app/`, and, only while you have a phone or tablet pairing (see "Devices" below), the list of devices at `https://gravitasmap.com/api/room` and, only when you press **Link a browser**, one request for a one-time link code at the same address (see "Link code" below), and, only when you press **Send** in About > Send feedback, one request to `https://gravitasmap.com/api/report` (see "Send feedback" below). The map windows below load the website like a browser. The update check is a plain GET of `https://gravitasmap.com/gravitas-app/version.json` when the app opens and every few hours. It sends no
name, no ID and no data about you (the user agent says only `GRAVITAS-app/<version>`). The answer can say one thing: that a newer version exists. That file
is signed (ed25519, the public key is inside the app) and the installer's SHA-256 is in it: an unsigned or changed file is ignored. A small update installs only
as the app is opened; while it is open the app only says "new version" and you press Update. The server can never change how the app behaves, switch
features on or off, or send code or settings. The code is open (app/src-tauri/src/updater.rs).

The installer carries Microsoft's small WebView2 bootstrapper inside it (nothing is downloaded by the installer for it from us; the bootstrapper itself may contact Microsoft when it installs the runtime). If the app starts on a PC where the WebView2 runtime is missing or unusable, it shows one message box in your Windows language (English when that language is not one of the app's six) and opens a Microsoft download page (`https://go.microsoft.com/fwlink/p/?LinkId=2124703`) in your default browser only if you click Yes; the app itself makes no network call for this (`app/src-tauri/src/webview_check.rs`).

While Thargoids are about (a Thargoid music track, a Nonhuman signal source or a systems shutdown in your Journal) the app writes a recording of what Status.json shows and of the Journal
events that matter into a file on your own computer (`%APPDATA%\com.gravitasmap.app\thargoid-rec\`), so a fight can be studied afterwards. These files never leave your computer
and are never sent anywhere: you can read, copy or delete them yourself (app/src-tauri/src/thargoid_rec.rs). One recording stops growing at 20 MB, and only the newest 30 recordings are kept (the app removes older ones).

**Travel history (Visits page, `app/src-tauri/src/visits.rs`, `visits/parse.rs`, `visits/arch.rs`, `visits/summary.rs`, `visits/hist.rs`, `visits/scan.rs`).** The app keeps a list of the star systems you arrived at, so you can look back at where you have been. It is built from the Journal files on your computer (jumps, carrier jumps and the place the game started in) and saved as an archive in the folder `visits\` inside the app's data folder (`%APPDATA%\com.gravitasmap.appisits\`): one file per month (`m-YYYY-MM.json`, UTC) and a small `index.json`. A month file holds, for each arrival, the Journal's time (UTC), the system name, the system's place in the galaxy (x, y, z in light years), how you arrived (jump, carrier jump or game start), and what you did there: docked (station name), sold or bought (commodity name, amount, credits, station name), sold exploration or organic data (credits, station), redeemed a voucher (credits), completed missions (how many, credits), and whether the arrival was a taxi ride or as a crew member. The index holds, for each Journal file, its name (a date), size and a few facts needed to read the next file (the system you were in and up to 12 station names with their market numbers), and for each month a summary: the number of arrivals and systems, first and last time, credits earned, light years flown, the five best-selling and most-bought goods, the five most-visited stations, and a coarse density map (how many arrivals fell into each 500 light-year cell of the galaxy). Nothing in the archive holds a commander name, a FID, a ship name, a mission id or free text. The Journal itself is not copied: only these facts are. The files stay on your computer, are never uploaded and are never sent to the map window or to any server; the Visits page loads one month at a time from them (the newest twelve months, and an older month only when you open it), and the search reads the month files on your computer. The archive is only a cache of facts from your Journals: it is rebuilt from them when it is missing or damaged, and the history is kept even after the game removes an old Journal. There is no second backup copy of the archive. Older versions kept one file, `visits.json` (with a copy in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\GRAVITAS-backup\`): it is not used any more and is deleted, with its copy, once the archive is complete. You can switch the history off (setting `visits_keep`, on by default: nothing is then read for it or written), and the **Forget** button deletes the whole `visits\` folder, the old `visits.json` and its copy. You can also delete them by hand.

If the app cannot read one of its own files (settings, Past combats, Past Thargoid, Debrief), it keeps the unreadable file next to it as `<name>.bad-<time>` and uses its backup copy. Both stay on your computer.

**Keyboard and mouse.** The app looks at the state of some keys and at the mouse, for three jobs. It uses the Windows calls GetAsyncKeyState, GetRawInputData and GetCursorPos, so some
antivirus programs may mention "input" calls: this list is what they are used for.
(1) *The app's own jobs, always:* the keys of the app's own shortcuts (the menu key and the others you set on the Keys page, `hotkeys.rs`), the panel keys of the game's Bindings file (a read-only
file) to guess which cockpit panel you opened (`panelkeys.rs`), your Free look button (`controls.rs`, and `rawmouse.rs`, which reads only the mouse buttons for that), and the cursor position to see
whether the game's own Esc menu is open or while you drag a window in Edit HUD (`overlay.rs`, `live.rs`). None of these is recorded.
(2) *Debrief mode, only if you switch it on (off by default):* it follows the keys that the game's own Bindings file binds to SHIP actions (Forward Thrust, Flight Assist, Boost, Primary Fire...) and
the mouse's movement, buttons and wheel, about every 8 ms, so that a window can show what you press and a report can show how a fight went (`inputs.rs`, `debrief.rs`). It looks ONLY while the game is
in front, you are in your ship (not the fighter, the SRV or on foot), and no panel or menu is open. No other key is ever looked at: a key that is not bound to a ship action is not read, so what you
type in chat or in another program is never seen. Outside those moments nothing is read at all. For each fight or hunt the app keeps, on your computer only (`debriefs.json` in the app's data
folder, the last 30, a copy in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\GRAVITAS-backup\`): when each of those ship keys went down and up, the mouse's movement added up in half-second slices (no cursor positions), and what the game
wrote about the fight (attacks, shields, hull steps, kills, the ship you locked, Flight Assist, overheating). It is never sent anywhere. You can read, copy or delete it: Past debriefs lists the records and
has a Delete button (and the file can be deleted by hand). Turning Debrief off stops all of it at once.
(3) *Nothing is sent to the game:* the app sends no keys to the game. The only key presses it sends are key-ups for Ctrl, Shift, Alt, Win and Esc when you close its menu, so none of them stays stuck (`menu.rs`).

**PVP mode.** Off until you switch it on. While it is on, the app makes one file for each fight with another commander, from what the game writes about YOU (`pvp.rs`, `pvpstore.rs`):
`XXX_vs_YYY_fight_N.gravitas` in `%APPDATA%\com.gravitasmap.app\pvp\` (XXX is you, YYY the other commander, N the fight's number). The file is plain text (JSON) that you can open in any editor.
It holds: your commander name and the other commander's name (and the commander names seen in the fight), the result (won, lost, no result), the Journal's own times (UTC) of the start and the end, the type of your ship, and what the game wrote
during the fight: when you were attacked, your shields and Flight Assist, your hull steps, overheating, your power distribution, the ships you locked and what a scan showed of their shield and hull, kills and their rewards,
and when you died. If the switch "Put my ship's build in the file" is on (it is on by default), also your ship's modules with their engineering (no ship name, no ident, no values).
It holds NO keys and NO mouse data (PVP mode never reads them), no commander ID, no credits, no system name. The files are never sent anywhere by the app: you send one to the other commander yourself, if you want to,
and you can delete them in Explorer. A file can be edited, so it is a record for learning, not proof. A file that comes from somewhere else is read only if it has exactly the shape of a PVP file (and is smaller than 4 MB), and nothing in it is ever run.
The Compare tab of the PVP page reads the files you pick (your saved ones, and files you choose with the file dialog, for instance the ones the other commander sent you) to match the fights that are the same fight and to show the two together
(`pvpsync.rs`). They are read in this window and by the app on your computer, and are not copied, changed, uploaded or sent anywhere. The two PCs' clocks are put together with a death and a kill (one moment), or an interdiction both files wrote, else as they are;
you can move one side by hand. Every name in a file is shown as plain text.

Your own files (settings, Past combats, Past Thargoid and BGS Watch data) get a second copy in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\GRAVITAS-backup\`, outside the app's data folder and the install folder, so
that an uninstall or a repair does not take your settings with it. The copies stay on your computer, are never sent anywhere and can be deleted by you at any time
(app/src-tauri/src/store.rs).

The map: the app's map is the website gravitasmap.com shown in a window of its own, and, if you switch it on (off by default), its System map alone as a window over the game (the page is drawn in a window far off the screen that nobody sees, and the app takes its
picture to draw it like its other windows). Both open gravitasmap.com like a browser would, so the usual website calls happen (the page, and the system data from our server and Spansh, see "Check it yourself"). The map window
asks for the system you are in, so **the name of that system is sent to gravitasmap.com** (in the web address), exactly as when you type it in the site. Nothing else from your
Journal goes out to the server by this. That page gets no power over the app: it cannot call any of its commands, the app only sends it small instructions (the system name, move, zoom) and, for the map window only, the Sync hand-over described below, and reads nothing back
(app/src-tauri/src/mapwin.rs, src/js/48-app-hook.js). The hidden System map window gets only the system name and the key instructions, never the Sync hand-over.

**Sync in the app's map window (v0.9.18).** The map window follows your jumps without asking for a Journal folder: the app reads the folder itself (it does anyway) and, only while the map window is open,
hands the window (1) the Journal lines whose event the site's own Sync needs (the list is the same as `SYNC_EV` in `src/js/30-sync.js`), the end of the newest Journal when the window opens and then each new line, and
(2) `Status.json` and `NavRoute.json` when they change. **Before a line is handed over the app removes what the page does not need** (`scrub` in `app/src-tauri/src/mapfeed.rs`, with a test): in every line the commander ID (`FID`) if present; LoadGame keeps only commander name, ship type, ship name and ident (credits, loan, game mode and the ID are removed); ReceiveText (chat) keeps only the channel for player and local chat (the text and the sender are removed), the channel and the sender name for wing lines (the text is removed, the sender is used to count each teammate's lines), and for NPC lines the sender, the game's own code word and the NPC's text (used for the Combat report); Statistics keeps only five combat counters, Rank and Progress only the combat value, Missions only each active mission's name and whether it expires, CarrierStats only carrier ID, type, name and callsign (no balance or crew). All other fields of the listed events are handed over as the game wrote them, and the page uses only what Sync needs. Lines of other events (Commander, Materials, Engineers, Market, Shipyard and the rest) stay in the app.
This goes into the page with webview `eval`, inside the window, and is repeated a few times (after 4 s, 12 s and then every minute) in case the page loaded late.
The app itself sends none of it to any server (`app/src-tauri/src/mapfeed.rs`, a test checks that its event list equals the site's), and the app keeps none of it: it is held in memory while the window is open and is gone when the window closes.
The map window is the same page as in a browser, so it keeps what that page keeps in a browser, here in the app's WebView2 storage on your computer (for example the last 30 combat reports, the name of your own carrier, recent journeys and searches),
and it sends something to our server only when you switch it on inside that window: phone pairing, the team board or Report a problem, and then only what this file lists for those features. Searches and system names go out as in a browser.
The trust is the same as choosing the Journal folder in a browser. Close the window and nothing is handed over any more. The tools in that window (Collisions, Sonification, Lenses) run on the same page.

**Send feedback (About page, `app/src-tauri/src/feedback.rs`).** A window where you write to the developers. Nothing is sent until you press **Send**, and then one request goes to `https://gravitasmap.com/api/report` (the same place as the site's Report a problem, handled by `functions/api/report.js`, mailed to the developers). It holds: the text you wrote, the kind you picked (problem, idea or question), your CMDR name and e-mail only if you typed them, the screenshots you added (at most 3, made smaller on your PC first), the app version and your Windows version, and the diagnostic report text only while the box for it is ticked (you can read it in the window first). It never includes your Journal, your FID or your settings. The server keeps your address only as a one-way code for one minute to limit repeat sends, the same as for site reports.

**Settings export and import (Settings page).** Export writes `GRAVITAS-settings-<date>.json` into your Documents folder. It holds your settings, including the BGS Watch screenshot folder path, but not the phone pairing, the update state, the Claims reset line, the BGS snapshots or the list of system names. Nothing is sent anywhere. Import first copies your current settings to `settings.json.before-import` in the app's data folder.

**BGS Watch (a mode of the app, off by default, `app/src-tauri/src/bgs.rs` and `bgs/`).** When you switch it on and press **Check the screenshots**, the app reads the newest N picture files named `Screenshot...` (BMP or PNG, N is your setting, they are the ones the game writes when you press F10) in the game's screenshot folder (`Pictures\Frontier Developments\Elite Dangerous`, or the folder you set). From the four newest Journal files it reads only the `Screenshot` lines (file name and time), to find the time of each picture, and ignores every other line. Files are opened for reading only: the app never writes, moves, renames, locks or deletes anything in a game folder or the screenshot folder. The pictures are processed in memory and are not copied, kept or sent anywhere. When you start the scan from the GRAVITAS menu over the game, the answer (the numbers and the small crops of the cells to check) waits in the app's memory until the GRAVITAS window takes it to show the confirm table, once; it is never written to disk. What you confirm is saved on this PC only: in the folder `bgs\` inside the app's data folder, one file per snapshot (`snap-<time>.json`) and a small `index.json`. A snapshot holds the system names, the influence percentages (and whether a row has a star), the time, the file names of the screenshots it came from, the picture size and how sure the reader was. Never the pictures. The list of system names is `bgs_names.json` in the app's data folder itself. Like your settings, these files also get a second copy in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\GRAVITAS-backup\` (not in the Store build). When the answer of a scan shows a cell the reader doubts, the app cuts a small picture of that cell out of the screenshot in memory and hands it to the window for that one answer: it is never written to a file and never kept. When you correct a cell and confirm it, the app can keep the shape of the digits or letters it had read wrong (a grey 36 x 40 bitmap of one glyph with its character, a few hundred bytes each, at most 400 per font, no pictures and no screenshot) in `bgs_learned.bin` in the app's data folder, with a second copy like the other files, and uses them to read better next time. A setting turns this off. A snapshot you save while some rows are still marked uncertain is stored with a note on those rows (`accepted_unchecked`). The button **Forget all BGS data** deletes every saved snapshot, the list of system names, the learned glyph shapes and their backup copies. Your BGS settings (on or off, screenshot folder, thresholds) stay in the settings until you change them. Nothing runs in the background: reading starts only when you press the button.
BGS Watch makes one network call, and only while its **Show the population next to the systems** switch is on (it is on by default; BGS Watch itself is off by default, and the call happens only after you switched BGS Watch on and have a snapshot). The app then sends one request per 100 system names (the systems of your newest snapshot) to Spansh, `https://spansh.co.uk/api/systems/search`, and keeps the answer (the population of each system) in `bgs_pop.json` in the app's data folder, asking again after 6 hours at the earliest. Only the system names go out: no CMDR name, no Journal data, no influence values, no screenshots. Spansh sees your address like any web server does. With the switch off the app makes no call at all. Apart from this, BGS Watch makes no network call. To give it the names of your faction's systems you can open the faction's page on Inara in your own browser, press its EXPORT button and load the CSV file you get into BGS Watch (a file you choose from your own disk). The app reads that file in memory and keeps only the "Star system" column (the system names) in `bgs_names.json`. Every other column (population, influence, updated and the rest) is dropped at once: it is not stored, not logged and not shown. The file itself is not copied or kept, and nothing is uploaded. You can also type or paste the names yourself.

BGS Watch can also **import a CSV** that its own Export CSV button wrote, for example from a friend who read the screenshots on his PC (a file you choose from your own disk). The app reads the file in memory and uses only four columns: the time of the snapshot, the system name, the influence percentage and the "confirmed" flag. The "change" column and any other column are ignored and never stored. You first see a summary (how many snapshots are new, which are already here, which names are not in your list) and nothing is saved until you confirm. The snapshots are stored like your own, marked as imported, at the time written in the file. The file itself is not copied or kept, and nothing is uploaded. Import CSV and Forget all BGS data work on the same saved snapshots.

**Diagnostic report (About page).** The app counts, in memory only, what the game writes that this version does not know (`app/src-tauri/src/drift.rs`): Journal lines read and lines that could not be read, event names and music track names it has no use for, Status.json reads and whether its Flags were missing or odd, flag bits and GuiFocus values outside the documented ones, time stamps it could not read, a few key fields that were missing, whether the Bindings file was found, and the first three error messages of the combat engine (first line only). The counters are never sent anywhere and never written to a file (the one line `gravitas.log` gets when Status.json has no usable Flags names no value). They are gone when the app closes. The button "Copy diagnostic report" puts a text of these counts on your clipboard, with the app version, the game version, build, language and the Windows version, and whether the Journal folder and the Bindings were found. It holds no commander name, no system or station names and no file paths. You decide whether to paste it anywhere. The Modes page shows a one-line note when something unknown was counted (the page asks the app for a yes or no, locally, at most every 10 seconds).

## What we cannot see or control

- **Hosting.** GRAVITAS runs on Cloudflare. Like any host, Cloudflare handles your IP address to deliver the pages and keeps short technical logs of the requests. Those requests include the names of the systems your map asks for, so in Cloudflare's logs a series of them could show where you travelled. GRAVITAS itself does not keep these, and does not read them to follow anyone.
- **Your own computer.** Browser extensions and security software can add their own code to any page. The site's rules tell your browser to block scripts that are not GRAVITAS's own, but we cannot answer for what runs outside the site.

## Check it yourself, in your browser

You do not need to read code to check this.

1. Open https://gravitasmap.com in Chrome or Edge.
2. Press **F12** and open the **Network** tab.
3. Turn on **Sync with the game** and play for a while: jump, dock, fight.
4. Watch the list. You will see:
   - `system?name=...` when you arrive in a new system,
   - the site's own files (`app.*.js`, pictures, sounds),
   - and only if you switched them on: `room` (pairing, team board) and `report`.
5. Click any request and look at its URL or **Payload**. You will not find a journal line.

Every network call the app makes is a `fetch(` or `new WebSocket(` in `src/js/`, plus the `import(` that loads the Lenses file. You can search for them. The site's Content-Security-Policy (`tools/_headers`) tells the browser to run only GRAVITAS's own code and the 3D library, and to connect only to gravitasmap.com.

## The Lenses and System Sonification

The Lenses and System Sonification (`src/lazy/lens.js`, in GRAVITAS MODES) do not read your journal and make no network call of their own. They load as one more of the site's own files when you open them. Every pattern, picture and note is worked out and played in your browser from the system's orbit data, which the map has already loaded. The melody card, the sound file (WAV) and the notes (MIDI) are made in your browser and saved only where you save them. The volume and the Studio's effects are kept in your browser (`localStorage`).
